School Culture, Vacations and Student Life in Korea 🇰🇷

School life in South Korea includes more than lessons and examinations. Students experience school traditions, extracurricular activities, school meals and different vacation periods. Some aspects are regulated nationally, while others depend on individual schools.

1. How Long Are School Vacations?

South Korean schools generally have two main vacation periods during the academic year. The school year begins in March, and the second semester starts at the end of August.

☀️ Summer Vacation

July – August

Summer vacation usually takes place during the warmest months of the year.

❄️ Winter Vacation

December – February

Winter vacation takes place around the end of the calendar year and the beginning of the next year.

Did you know? The exact vacation dates and duration can differ between schools. The Ministry of Education gives these months as the typical vacation periods.

2. Do Students Wear School Uniforms?

School uniforms are a familiar part of Korean school culture. However, the Ministry of Education's general English-language information does not establish one nationwide uniform requirement for all schools.

Uniform policies can depend on the individual school. Therefore, students' clothing requirements may differ from one school to another.

Important: Korean school uniforms are also widely recognized through Korean popular culture, but television dramas should not be treated as proof of the rules followed by every real school.

3. Are Students Allowed to Use Smartphones at School?

South Korea introduced a national legal restriction on students' use of smartphones and other smart devices during lessons.

📵 Smartphone Restrictions

Effective March 1, 2026

  • Students may not use smartphones or other smart devices during class.
  • Exceptions are possible for educational purposes, accessibility needs or emergency situations, when permitted by the school or teacher.
  • Schools can also restrict the use or possession of devices on school premises.

This means students cannot freely use their phones during lessons. Individual schools may introduce additional restrictions through their own school rules.

4. What Do Students Eat at School?

South Korean schools provide school meals for students. The Ministry of Education monitors food safety and has specifically published information about safety checks for seafood used in school lunches.

The exact menu is not the same in every school. Meals and ingredients can vary depending on the school's menu and arrangements.

Food safety: The Korean government carries out checks at different stages of seafood production and distribution to help ensure that seafood served in school meals is safe.

5. What Kinds of Clubs and Extracurricular Activities Are Available?

Korean schools can offer activities outside the regular curriculum. The Ministry of Education has documented several examples of after-school programs and student activities.

⚽ Sports Clubs

Students can participate in sports and physical education activities. Some school sports clubs also take part in events beyond their own schools.

🎨 Arts Activities

Arts programs give students opportunities to explore creative interests and develop artistic abilities.

🤖 Robotics Classes

Robotics is one example of an after-school activity that can help students develop technical and creative skills.

🌟 Creative Programs

Schools can offer additional activities designed to develop students' interests, talents and abilities.

National School Sports Club Festival: The Ministry of Education has organized a national festival where school sports clubs can participate in sporting activities and competitions.

6. What Kinds of School Festivals or Field Trips Are Common?

Schools can organize experiential learning activities and educational field trips. These allow students to gain practical experiences outside ordinary classroom lessons.

  • Educational field trips: Students can visit places connected to their school subjects and learning activities.
  • Science-related experiences: The Ministry has documented field trips and practical learning experiences in science-focused education.
  • Sports events: School sports clubs can participate in organized competitions and festivals.
The Ministry's English-language information does not provide a complete nationwide list of school festivals. Events and traditions can differ between schools.

7. How Formal Is the Relationship Between Teachers and Students?

Respect for teachers is an important topic in South Korea's education policies. The Ministry of Education emphasizes the importance of protecting teachers' rights and creating a respectful school environment.

The Ministry also highlights the importance of communication and mutual respect between students, parents and teachers.

Important values:
  • Respect between students and teachers
  • Trust and communication
  • Protection of teachers' educational activities
  • A positive and safe school environment

The exact level of formality in everyday interactions can vary. Official policy information does not establish one universal way in which all teachers and students communicate.

8. What Do Students Usually Do in Their Free Time?

Students' free-time routines can differ depending on their interests, school schedules and personal circumstances.

The Ministry of Education documents several after-school activities, including:

  • Sports and physical activities
  • Arts and creative programs
  • Robotics and technical classes
  • Other after-school learning activities
Note: The Ministry's general English-language sources do not provide representative statistics about how Korean students spend all of their free time. It is therefore better not to assume that every student follows the same daily routine.

Key Facts to Remember

  • Summer vacation is typically in July and August.
  • Winter vacation is typically in December and February.
  • Uniform policies can differ between schools.
  • Smartphone use during lessons is nationally restricted from March 1, 2026.
  • Schools provide meals and monitor food safety.
  • Sports, arts and robotics are examples of extracurricular activities.
  • Respect and communication between teachers, students and parents are emphasized by the Ministry of Education.
