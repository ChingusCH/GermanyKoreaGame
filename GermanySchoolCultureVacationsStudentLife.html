School Culture, Vacations and Student Life in Germany

School life in Germany is shaped by regional traditions, school policies and individual interests. Students have regular lessons, holidays and opportunities to participate in extracurricular activities.

However, some aspects of school life differ between the 16 federal states (Bundesländer) and individual schools. The following information describes common features of German schools, with examples from North Rhine-Westphalia (NRW) where appropriate.

🏖️ School Vacations

How long are school vacations?

German students have several school holiday periods throughout the year. These usually include:

  • Summer holidays: Usually six weeks.
  • Autumn holidays: Usually one or two weeks.
  • Christmas holidays: Usually around two weeks.
  • Easter or spring holidays: Usually one or two weeks.
  • Additional holidays: Some federal states have winter or Pentecost holidays.

The exact dates and duration depend on the federal state. The summer holidays are staggered between the states so that not everyone starts their holidays at the same time.

According to the German Standing Conference of the Ministers of Education (KMK), the total holiday period amounts to 75 working days per school year, including 12 Saturdays.

In NRW, for example, students usually have summer, autumn, Christmas and Easter holidays, as well as additional individual days off.

👕 School Uniforms

Do students wear school uniforms?

In Germany, school uniforms are generally not common. Most students wear their own everyday clothes to school.

There is usually no nationwide uniform requirement. Students can normally choose their own clothing, as long as it follows the school's general rules.

Some schools may introduce a shared dress code or recommend uniform school clothing. In NRW, for example, the school conference can recommend uniform clothing under certain conditions, including the agreement of the student representatives.

Overall, wearing personal clothing is the normal experience for most students in Germany.

📱 Smartphones at School

Are students allowed to use smartphones at school?

Smartphone rules in Germany depend on the federal state and the individual school. There is no single nationwide rule that applies identically to every school.

In many schools, students are not allowed to use their phones during lessons unless the teacher permits it for educational purposes.

Some schools also restrict smartphone use during breaks or throughout the entire school day. Other schools have different rules depending on the students' age.

In NRW, schools have been encouraged to develop clear, age-appropriate rules for smartphones and smartwatches. A survey published in January 2026 reported that around 98% of public schools in the state had established binding smartphone rules.

Therefore, whether students can use their phones during breaks or after lessons depends on the rules of their particular school.

🍽️ School Meals

What do students eat at school?

German schools can offer different food options. What students eat depends on the school, its facilities and whether it provides a lunch service.

At many schools, especially those with all-day programmes (Ganztagsschulen), students can have lunch in a school canteen (Mensa).

Typical school lunch options may include:

  • Pasta with tomato sauce or other sauces
  • Potatoes with vegetables
  • Rice dishes
  • Meat or fish with side dishes
  • Vegetarian meals
  • Salads, fruit and desserts

The exact menu changes from day to day. Many students also bring their own food from home, commonly known as a Pausenbrot.

This might include sandwiches, fruit, vegetables, yoghurt or other snacks.

The German Nutrition Society (DGE) provides quality standards for school catering. These offer guidance on balanced, healthy and sustainable school meals. However, not every school follows an identical menu.

⚽ Clubs and Extracurricular Activities

What kinds of clubs and extracurricular activities are available?

German schools can offer a variety of extracurricular activities, often organized as Arbeitsgemeinschaften (AGs). These activities allow students to explore interests outside regular lessons.

Examples of school clubs and activities include:

  • Sports: Football, basketball, volleyball, badminton and athletics.
  • Music: School bands, choirs, orchestras and instrumental groups.
  • Arts: Drawing, painting, photography and theatre.
  • Science and technology: Robotics, computer science and science projects.
  • Languages: Language clubs and exchange activities.
  • Social activities: Student councils, environmental groups and school newspapers.

The activities available depend on the school, its facilities and the teachers or external partners who organize them.

Students can also join sports clubs, music schools and other organizations outside school.

🎉 School Festivals and Field Trips

What kinds of school festivals or field trips are common?

School life in Germany includes events that bring students, teachers and sometimes parents together. These activities can be educational, cultural or simply social.

Common examples include:

  • School festivals (Schulfeste): Events with food, games, performances and activities organized by students and teachers.
  • Sports days (Sportfeste): Events involving athletics, team sports and other physical activities.
  • Christmas events: Some schools organize Christmas celebrations, concerts or seasonal markets.
  • Class trips (Klassenfahrten): Multi-day trips where students travel together and participate in shared activities.
  • Excursions (Exkursionen): Visits to museums, historical sites, nature areas, theatres or other educational locations.
  • Educational visits: Trips to companies, universities, exhibitions or scientific institutions.

Class trips are an important part of school life. They allow students to experience new places, develop independence and strengthen relationships with classmates.

The exact events and trips vary from school to school. There is no single nationwide calendar of school festivals.

👩‍🏫 Teacher-Student Relationships

How formal is the relationship between teachers and students?

Teacher-student relationships in Germany are generally based on mutual respect, clear responsibilities and communication.

Students are usually expected to address teachers politely. In many schools, students use "Frau" or "Herr" followed by the teacher's surname.

Whether students use the informal "du" or the formal "Sie" depends on the school and its traditions. At many schools, students address teachers formally, while other schools use a more informal approach.

Teachers are responsible for guiding students, explaining subject material and maintaining a productive learning environment. Students are expected to participate, follow school rules and take responsibility for their learning.

The relationship can become less formal as students get older, especially in upper secondary education. However, there is no single rule that applies to every German school.

🎮 Free Time and Student Life

What do students usually do in their free time?

German students have different hobbies and interests. Their free time is often spent with friends, family or in organized activities.

Common leisure activities include:

  • Playing sports in clubs or with friends
  • Listening to music or playing instruments
  • Gaming and using social media
  • Watching films and series
  • Reading books
  • Meeting friends, going shopping or visiting cafés
  • Going cycling, skating or spending time outdoors
  • Participating in volunteer work or youth organizations

Sports clubs are particularly popular in Germany. Football, for example, is one of the most widely practiced sports among young people.

Digital media also plays an important role in everyday life. Many young people use messaging services, social media and online entertainment.

However, there is no single typical free-time routine. Students' activities depend on their age, interests, location, family and available opportunities.

🌍 School Life in Germany

Overall, German school life combines academic education with social activities, personal interests and extracurricular opportunities.

Compared with countries where school uniforms are common, German students generally have more freedom to choose their everyday clothing. Smartphone rules, holiday dates and extracurricular programmes also vary between schools and federal states.

School is therefore not only a place for learning, but also an important environment for friendships, personal development and shared experiences.
