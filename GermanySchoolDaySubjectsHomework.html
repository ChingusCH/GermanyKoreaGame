School Day, Subjects & Homework in Germany

School life in Germany can differ depending on the federal state, school type, and individual school. However, there are some common features of a typical German school day.

🏫 School Day

When does school begin and end?

In Germany, school usually begins at around 8:00 AM. The end of the school day varies depending on the school and the student's timetable.

Many students finish their regular lessons around 1:00–5:00 PM, while students at schools with longer schedules may stay until the afternoon.

How many lessons do students have per day?

Students usually have around 5–8 lessons per day. A lesson normally lasts about 45 minutes, although some schools use longer lesson periods.

Students have breaks between lessons. Younger students generally have shorter school days, while older students may have more lessons depending on their timetable.

📚 School Subjects

Which subjects are compulsory?

Compulsory subjects vary somewhat between Germany's federal states and school types. Common subjects include:

  • Mathematics
  • German
  • Foreign languages
  • Science subjects
  • History
  • Geography
  • Physical Education
  • Art and/or Music
  • Religious Education or Ethics, depending on the school

Which subjects can students choose?

As students get older, they usually have more opportunities to choose subjects. The exact choices depend on the school and federal state.

In upper secondary school, students can often choose elective subjects, advanced courses, or different subject combinations. Some schools also offer subjects such as additional languages, computer science, economics, or specialized courses.

📝 Homework

How much homework do students receive?

The amount of homework in Germany differs greatly. It depends on the school, grade level, teachers, and subjects.

Students may receive exercises in mathematics, reading or writing assignments, preparation for lessons, vocabulary learning, or longer projects.

How much time do students spend studying after school?

There is no fixed amount of study time for every student. Students may spend anywhere from a short amount of time to several hours studying or completing homework, especially before important tests.

👩‍🏫 Private Tutoring

Do students attend private academies or tutoring?

Some German students receive private tutoring (Nachhilfe), especially when they have difficulties in subjects such as mathematics, German, or foreign languages.

Tutoring can take place individually, in small groups, or through specialized tutoring organizations. However, not every student receives private tutoring.

🌍 Languages

Which languages do students learn?

English is the most common foreign language taught in German schools. Depending on the school, students may later choose or study additional languages.

Common choices include French, Spanish and Latin. Some schools also offer languages such as Italian, Russian, or other languages.

Language choices can depend heavily on the school and the student's educational pathway.

🎓 School Life

German students generally have a mixture of lessons, breaks, homework, tests, and extracurricular activities. Depending on the school, students may also participate in sports clubs, music groups, school trips, student councils, or other activities.

Overall, the German school system gives students increasing choice and responsibility as they move into the higher grades.
