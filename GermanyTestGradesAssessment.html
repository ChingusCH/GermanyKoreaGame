Exams, Grades and Assessment in Germany

Examinations and grades are an important part of school life in Germany. Students are assessed through written tests, oral contributions, practical work and other assignments.

However, the exact rules can differ between Germany's 16 federal states (Bundesländer). The following information describes general features of the German system and uses North Rhine-Westphalia (NRW) as a concrete example where necessary.

📝 Types of Tests and Examinations

What kinds of tests or exams do students take?

German students take different types of assessments throughout their school years. These can include:

  • Klassenarbeiten: Formal written tests, often in subjects such as mathematics, German and foreign languages.
  • Tests and short written exercises: Shorter assessments that check students' understanding of recently studied topics.
  • Oral assessments: Contributions during lessons, discussions, explanations and answering questions.
  • Presentations: Individual or group presentations about specific topics.
  • Projects and practical assignments: Work that allows students to demonstrate their knowledge through practical or creative tasks.
  • Final examinations: Important examinations that may be required to obtain certain school-leaving qualifications.

In the final years of the Gymnasium, students also take the Abitur examinations, which are an important part of obtaining the general university entrance qualification.

📅 Frequency of Examinations

How often do students take exams?

There is no single nationwide number of examinations that every German student must take. The frequency depends on the federal state, school type, grade level and subject.

Students usually have several written assessments throughout a school year. Subjects such as mathematics, German and foreign languages often include regular written tests.

In addition, students are assessed through oral contributions, practical work and other assignments during lessons. These assessments take place throughout the school year, not only during formal examination periods.

In NRW, the regulations specify how written work and other classroom performance are included in assessment. However, the exact number of written tests depends on the school level and subject.

📊 The German Grading System

How are grades calculated?

In Germany, grades are generally based on a combination of different types of performance. In NRW, the two main assessment areas are:

  • Written performance: Class tests, examinations and other written assignments.
  • Other classroom performance: Oral contributions, practical work, presentations and other activities connected to lessons.

Both assessment areas must be appropriately considered when determining the overall grade. Their exact weighting can depend on the school level, subject and applicable regulations.

In the upper secondary school system in NRW (gymnasiale Oberstufe), courses with written examinations generally combine the results from written exams (Klausuren) and other classroom performance (Sonstige Mitarbeit).

These two assessment areas are normally given equal consideration when determining the final course grade. The teacher must also consider the student's overall development during the semester rather than simply calculating an arithmetic average.

Are class participation, speaking activities and presentations included?

Yes. In Germany, assessment is not based only on written examinations.

In NRW, oral and practical contributions are explicitly included in the assessment category Sonstige Leistungen. This can include:

  • Answering questions during lessons
  • Participating in classroom discussions
  • Explaining ideas and solving problems orally
  • Giving individual or group presentations
  • Completing practical assignments
  • Participating in other subject-related activities

The exact assessment methods depend on the subject and school level. For example, speaking activities can be particularly relevant in foreign language lessons.

🔢 Grades: Highest and Lowest Marks

What are the highest and lowest grades students can receive?

In most German school years, the traditional grading scale ranges from 1 to 6. A grade of 1 is the best, while 6 is the worst.

  • 1 – Very good (sehr gut)
  • 2 – Good (gut)
  • 3 – Satisfactory (befriedigend)
  • 4 – Sufficient (ausreichend)
  • 5 – Poor (mangelhaft)
  • 6 – Very poor (ungenügend)

A grade of 4 is generally considered a pass, while grades 5 and 6 indicate insufficient performance.

The 15-point system in upper secondary school

In the qualification phase of upper secondary education in NRW, students use a different grading system: the 0–15 point system.

  • 15 points: Very good plus
  • 14 points: Very good
  • 13 points: Very good minus
  • 10–12 points: Good
  • 7–9 points: Satisfactory
  • 4–6 points: Sufficient
  • 1–3 points: Poor
  • 0 points: Very poor

The points are used to calculate students' results during the qualification phase and contribute to their final Abitur grade.

🔄 Retaking Examinations

Can students retake exams?

The possibility of retaking an examination depends on the reason for the retake and the type of assessment.

If a student cannot take an examination for a valid reason, such as illness, the school may arrange a make-up examination (Nachschreibearbeit) or another form of assessment.

However, students generally do not have an automatic right to repeat every test simply because they received a low grade.

There are also special regulations for final examinations. In NRW, certain examinations can generally be repeated once if they have not been passed, subject to the applicable examination rules.

Therefore, it is important to distinguish between missing an examination for a valid reason, receiving a poor grade and failing a final examination. These situations are treated differently.

🎓 Importance of Grades for the Future

How important are grades for students' future education or careers?

Grades can have a significant influence on students' educational opportunities in Germany. They are especially important when students apply for certain university programmes.

The Abitur provides the general higher education entrance qualification. Its overall grade can be particularly important for competitive university programmes.

Some degree programmes have a Numerus Clausus (NC). This means that the number of available university places is limited, and the grades of applicants can influence who receives admission.

However, not every university programme uses an NC. Other admission requirements may include entrance tests, interviews, practical experience or specific qualifications.

Grades can also matter when applying for vocational training (Ausbildung). Employers may consider school certificates and subject grades when selecting applicants. Nevertheless, practical skills, motivation and personal qualifications can also play an important role.

⚖️ Academic Pressure: Germany vs. South Korea

Which school system puts more pressure on students?

It is difficult to say that one entire school system puts more pressure on every student. Academic pressure depends on individual circumstances, school expectations, family expectations, examinations and future educational goals.

South Korea is often associated with strong academic competition and additional studying outside regular school hours, including private academies known as hagwon.

In Germany, students also experience academic pressure, especially during important examinations such as the Abitur or when applying for competitive university programmes.

The OECD's PISA 2022 research provides one possible comparison: it found that mathematics anxiety had increased in many countries compared with 2012. In South Korea, mathematics anxiety had decreased significantly compared with 2012, while the OECD reported rising anxiety in many European countries.

However, mathematics anxiety is only one part of academic pressure. It does not measure every aspect of students' school lives, such as homework, private tutoring, family expectations or competition for university places.

Therefore, there is no single definitive answer. Both countries have students who experience considerable academic pressure, but the sources and forms of that pressure can differ.
