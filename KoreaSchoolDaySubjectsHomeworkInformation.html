School Day, Subjects & Homework in Korea

South Korean school life is often associated with a structured school day, strong academic expectations, and additional study outside regular school hours.

🏫 School Day

When does school begin and end?

A typical school day in Korea begins at around 8:00–9:00 AM. The exact schedule depends on the school and the student's age.

Regular lessons may finish in the afternoon, often around 2:00–4:00 PM. However, many students continue studying after their regular school day.

How many lessons do students have per day?

Students usually have several lessons each day, with the number depending on their grade and timetable. A school day generally contains around 6–8 class periods, with breaks between lessons.

📚 School Subjects

Which subjects are compulsory?

Korean students study a broad range of compulsory subjects. Common subjects include:

  • Korean language
  • Mathematics
  • Science
  • Social Studies / History
  • English
  • Physical Education
  • Arts and other school subjects

Which subjects can students choose?

Students generally gain more choice as they become older. In high school, students can select certain elective subjects depending on their school and educational program.

These choices can include additional sciences, mathematics, social studies, foreign languages, arts, technology, or other specialized subjects.

📝 Homework & Studying

How much homework do students receive?

Korean students can receive a considerable amount of homework and independent study. The exact amount varies by school, grade, and subject.

Homework can include exercises, reading, vocabulary, preparation for tests, projects, and revision of material learned in class.

How much time do students spend studying after school?

Studying often continues after regular school hours. Some students study at home, while others attend hagwon, private academies that provide additional lessons and preparation.

Especially during important examination periods, students may spend several additional hours studying after school.

🏫 Private Academies – Hagwon

Do students attend private academies or tutoring?

Yes. Private academies, known as hagwon (학원), are an important part of education in South Korea.

Students may attend hagwon for subjects such as mathematics, English, Korean, science, or other academic areas. Not every student attends a hagwon, but they are very common.

🌏 Languages

Which languages do students learn?

English is a major foreign language taught in Korean schools and is an important part of the education system.

Some students also study additional languages such as Chinese or Japanese. Learning Chinese characters can also be useful because Korean vocabulary contains many words with Chinese-origin roots, known as Sino-Korean vocabulary.

Korean students therefore may encounter Chinese characters (Hanja), although modern Korean is primarily written using Hangul.

🎓 School Life

School life in Korea can be academically demanding. Students often balance regular lessons with homework, self-study, extracurricular activities, and sometimes private academy classes.

At the same time, students also participate in sports, clubs, school events, friendships, and other activities. The experience can vary considerably depending on the student's age, school, location, and individual circumstances.
