Exams, Grades and Assessment in Korea 🇰🇷

Examinations and academic performance play an important role in South Korea's education system. Students are assessed through school-based evaluations, while the nationwide College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) is one of the factors considered for university admission.

1. What Kinds of Tests and Exams Do Students Take?

Students encounter different forms of assessment throughout their education. These assessments help schools evaluate academic progress and provide information for future educational pathways.

School Examinations

Schools use examinations to assess students' understanding of subjects and their academic performance.

School-Based Assessments

Students' academic development and performance are documented in official school records.

CSAT

The College Scholastic Ability Test is a nationwide standardized examination used as one factor in university admission.

Qualifying Examinations

Certain examinations can provide an alternative route for people seeking academic qualifications equivalent to completing a school level.

The CSAT – College Scholastic Ability Test

The CSAT is one of the most important examinations in South Korea's education system. Its results can influence students' opportunities when applying to universities.

Universities may consider CSAT scores together with school records and other admission requirements.

The examination is held as part of the annual university admissions cycle.

2. How Often Do Students Take Exams?

The South Korean Ministry of Education does not specify one nationwide timetable or an exact number of school examinations per year in its general English-language education information.

The frequency and timing of ordinary school examinations can depend on the school, grade and assessment arrangements.

Important distinction: Ordinary school examinations follow school assessment arrangements. The CSAT is a separate nationwide examination held once for each annual university admissions cycle.

3. How Are Grades Calculated?

Students' academic performance is documented in official school records. These records are important because they can be used during the selection process for higher education institutions.

The Ministry of Education has published guidelines concerning school records, subject evaluation and grade calculation.

  • Academic performance: Students' performance in their school subjects is documented.
  • Subject evaluation: Schools follow evaluation guidelines for their different subjects.
  • School records: Academic information is recorded and may be considered during university admission.
Note: There is no single universal formula in the Ministry's English-language overview that explains how every student's final grades are calculated.

4. Are Participation and Presentations Included?

South Korea's official school-record system is not limited to recording examination results. It also describes students' development and learning processes.

Teachers are expected to observe students and record verified information about their progress.

  • Learning development: Teachers document students' progress over time.
  • Academic achievement: Students' performance in their subjects is recorded.
  • Teacher observations: Official school records can include information based on teachers' observations of students.
The official sources consulted do not confirm that every school grades class participation, speaking activities and presentations in exactly the same way.

5. What Are the Highest and Lowest Grades?

South Korea does not have one single grading scale that can be applied to every school assessment and every examination.

The grading arrangements can differ depending on the subject and type of assessment.

Assessment Grading Information
Ordinary school assessments Grade calculation follows the relevant school and subject evaluation guidelines.
CSAT Scoring arrangements differ depending on the examination subject.
Some CSAT subjects A nine-grade system is used for certain subjects.
Important: It would be misleading to give one universal highest and lowest grade for the entire Korean school system. The grading scale depends on the assessment being discussed.

6. Can Students Retake Exams?

The Ministry's general English-language education pages do not specify a universal right to retake ordinary school examinations.

The CSAT and ordinary school examinations are different assessment systems. Their retake arrangements should not be treated as identical.

Not specified: The exact rules for retaking ordinary school examinations cannot be confirmed from the general Ministry sources used for this page.

7. How Important Are Grades for the Future?

Academic performance can have a significant influence on students' future educational opportunities.

According to the Ministry of Education, admission to higher education institutions can depend on several factors, including:

  • CSAT results: Scores from the nationwide university entrance examination.
  • School records: Students' academic performance and documented educational development.
  • University-specific requirements: Additional admission criteria depending on the institution and its selection process.
Why does this matter? Students' performance during high school can influence their opportunities to enter different universities and higher education programs.

8. Which School System Puts More Pressure on Students?

Examinations, school records and university admission are important parts of the Korean education system. However, the Ministry's official education information does not provide a direct comparison of student stress between South Korea and Germany.

To determine which country puts more pressure on students, reliable statistics about student stress, mental well-being and academic workload in both countries would be needed.

Conclusion: The available Ministry sources explain the importance of academic assessment in South Korea, but they do not establish which country has the more stressful school system.

Key Facts to Remember

  • South Korea uses school-based assessments and official school records.
  • The CSAT is a nationwide examination used as one factor in university admission.
  • University admission can depend on both CSAT results and school records.
  • The Ministry's general English-language pages do not establish one universal grading scale, exam frequency or retake policy for all schools.
