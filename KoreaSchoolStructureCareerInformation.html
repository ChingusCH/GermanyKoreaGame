School Structure and Educational Pathways in Korea 🇰🇷

South Korea has a structured education system that guides students from elementary school to higher education. The system is commonly known as the 6–3–3–4 system, referring to the number of years spent at each educational level.

1. The Korean Education System

Students generally complete 12 years of school before moving on to higher education. The education system consists of elementary, middle and high school, followed by university or other forms of higher education.

Important: Elementary and middle school education are compulsory in South Korea. High school and university are not compulsory.

2. What Types of Schools Are There?

South Korea has different types of schools, particularly at high-school level. Some provide general education, while others focus on specific academic or professional fields.

  • Elementary schools: Provide six years of primary education and establish students' basic academic knowledge.
  • Middle schools: Provide three years of lower secondary education. They can be public or private.
  • Regular high schools: Offer general education across a wide range of subjects.
  • Special-purpose high schools: Focus on particular fields, such as foreign languages, science, arts or physical education.
  • Vocational high schools: Emphasize practical training and professional skills to prepare students for specific careers.
  • Autonomous high schools: Have greater flexibility in organizing their educational programs.
  • Schools for gifted students: Provide specialized education for students with particular abilities and talents.

3. At What Age Do Students Move Between School Levels?

Students normally move to the next educational level after completing the previous one. The following table shows the typical age ranges published by the Korean Ministry of Education.

School Level Typical Age Range Duration
Elementary School 6–11 6 years
Middle School 12–14 3 years
High School 15–17 3 years

These are typical ages, not absolute rules for every student. Individual educational pathways may differ.

5. What Qualifications Do Students Receive?

Students progress through the educational system by completing the requirements of each school level.

  • Elementary school: After completing six years, students can move on to middle school.
  • Middle school: After completing three years, students can apply for high-school admission.
  • High school: Students who successfully complete high school graduate with a high-school graduation qualification.
  • University: Students who successfully complete a bachelor's program receive a bachelor's degree.

The Ministry also recognizes qualifying examinations and equivalent academic credentials for certain educational pathways.

6. How Do Students Enter University?

Students can enter higher education after graduating from high school or obtaining an equivalent academic qualification. University admission is determined by several factors.

  • College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT): A standardized examination whose scores can be considered during the university admission process.
  • School records: Students' academic performance and achievements during high school can also influence admission.
  • Other admission requirements: Universities may use additional criteria depending on their individual admission processes.

Higher Education Options

South Korea offers several types of higher education institutions. Students can choose a pathway depending on their academic goals and future careers.

  • Four-year universities: Offer bachelor's degree programs in many academic fields.
  • Junior colleges: Usually offer two- or three-year programs with a more practical and career-oriented focus.
  • Universities of Education: Focus on preparing future teachers.
  • Polytechnic colleges: Provide technical and vocational education.
  • Cyber and open universities: Offer alternative forms of higher education, including distance learning.
Junior colleges: Graduates can enter the workforce or, depending on the pathway and admission requirements, transfer to a four-year university.
